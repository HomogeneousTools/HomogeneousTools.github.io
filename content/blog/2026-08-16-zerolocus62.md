[ZeroLocus62](https://github.com/HomogeneousTools/ZeroLocus62) is a compact,
canonical encoding for bundles, zero loci, and degeneracy loci of completely
reducible vector bundles on partial flag varieties. The label
[`40.G`](https://zl62.homogeneous.tools/decode/40.G) is a quintic threefold, and
[`603.111`](https://zl62.homogeneous.tools/decode/603.111) is the
[Fano 3-fold 1-10](https://www.fanography.info/1-10), the zero locus of
$(\bigwedge^2 \mathcal{U}^\vee)^{\oplus 3}$ on $\mathrm{Gr}(3,7)$.

## The format

A label has the shape `<ambient>.<bundle>`. The part before the dot encodes a
product of partial flag varieties, one Dynkin type, rank, and set of marked
nodes per factor; the part after the dot encodes the highest weights of the
summands of the bundle. A third form with two `-` separators encodes degeneracy
loci. Everything is written in the Base62 alphabet, the 62 characters
`0`&ndash;`9`, `A`&ndash;`Z`, `a`&ndash;`z`, which is where the name comes
from: labels stay short, and they are safe in URLs and filenames without any
escaping.

The format is canonical: summands and ambient factors are put in a prescribed
order before encoding, so two systems describing the same zero locus produce
the same string. That is exactly what you want when storing them in a database
or communicating them across systems.

## Now in the General registry

The repository ships the specification (currently `v3.1.2`, for the v3.1
format) together with reference implementations in Python, Julia, JavaScript,
and Macaulay2. The Julia one is now in the General registry, so installing it
is simply

```julia
using Pkg
Pkg.add("ZeroLocus62")
```

instead of the `Pkg.add(url=..., subdir="julia")` incantation from the
[previous](/blog/2026/07/17/partialflagvarieties-pre-release/)
[posts](/blog/2026/08/13/partialflagvarieties-0-3-1/).

For the full specification, worked examples, and a live decoder, see the
[website](https://zl62.homogeneous.tools).
